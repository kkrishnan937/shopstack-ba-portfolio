01

The situation

ShopStack’s existing storefront supported browsing, login and add-to-cart requests. It did not give customers a place to confirm what had been saved or how much their selections cost.

I started with the current journey and the missing capabilities. As I explored quantity changes and removal, the questions became more specific: what happens if an update succeeds but its confirmation is lost? Can a retrieval failure be mistaken for an empty cart? Which actions are safe while the outcome is unknown?

THE QUESTION THAT SHAPED MY ANALYSIS

What can we accurately tell the customer when we don’t yet know what happened?

03

Decisions that mattered

A

A failed retrieval isn’t an empty cart

I specified distinct failure and empty states. A failed read must not claim a confirmed zero subtotal, and Retry must retrieve saved data without changing it.

Implemented and exercised
B

An unknown update isn’t a confirmed failure

I worked through duplicate-action risk and specified checking the original request before another update. The mutation recovery design remains outside the delivered slice.

Requirements defined · delivery open
C

Session expiry and cart expiry need different messages

I separated authentication recovery from the saved cart’s 30-day retention policy. Signing in again should not imply the customer’s cart expired.

Distinct states exercised · exact boundaries open
D

Start with a path we can actually verify

I defined cart viewing as the first implementation slice, with quantity changes, removal and logout invalidation behind explicit unresolved gates.

Scope and limitations recorded
04

From requirement to evidence

I kept the test outcome connected to the customer expectation: a retrieval retry must recover the saved cart without changing it.

THE EXECUTED RECOVERY SCENARIO
  1. Given two mugs at CAD 12.50 and one mouse at CAD 24.99 are saved.
  2. When the cart read fails, the customer sees an error and a Retry action.
  3. Then a successful retry shows the original selections and CAD 49.99. Saved-state snapshots remain unchanged.
ACTUAL APPLICATION CAPTURE

Recovery succeeds: the original saved selections and subtotal are displayed.

06

What I learned

Ask what is confirmed

I learned to separate a missing response from a failed action, and a proposed decision from an approved one.

Make the next decision clear

I practised identifying who needs to investigate, what evidence they should bring back, and which work can proceed independently.

Show the limits of the evidence

I learned that a small verified path is more useful than a larger completion claim that the results cannot support.

WHERE THIS CASE STUDY STOPS

I completed this as a BA portfolio exercise. Independent research and business acceptance remain outstanding. Quantity updates, removal, logout revocation, exact retention boundaries and full accessibility are not all implemented or verified. Production deployment is unconfirmed; the AWS workflow failed at credential setup.
